Rory Millard Engineer

Engineer working where probability meets the physical world.

I build systems that reason about real things under uncertainty: where a lost aircraft lies, which radar echo is a ship, when a search has done enough. The mathematics is Bayesian. The standard is that every number says how sure it is, and why.

ABYSSALResearch prototype · ongoing

Finding what is lost, with probability that cannot overclaim.A Bayesian workbench for searching for aircraft, ships and people. It keeps an honest map of where the target could be, learns from every empty search, and says where the next hour should go.

Belief
Log-space Bayes over space and time. Empty searches count as evidence; five retellings of one sighting count once.
Signals
Fourteen physics engines (radar, sonar, sound in the ocean, magnetics, drift) behind a single calibration gate.
Decisions
Where to search next, what that is worth, and when to stop.
Evidence
Maths tested against known answers; engines calibrated on answer keys; backtested on a real loss. Not yet used in a live search.
