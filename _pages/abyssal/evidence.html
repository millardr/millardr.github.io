Every study below runs the engine code the ABYSSAL app runs, on real public data, and scores it against an independent truth: something the engine never saw. Each one writes a results file with a receipt for every input (the URL and SHA-256 of the radar window, buoy file, AIS day or chart record) and a script that re-runs it. Where the truth is made rather than found, as with planted targets, it says so.

The numbers are not all flattering. That is the point: a detector is worth exactly its measured hit and false-alarm rates, and the weak ones are printed as large as the strong ones.

Wind from the radar · CMOD5.n

Reading the wind off the sea surface

Wind roughens the sea and the radar sees the roughness. The engine inverts the CMOD5.n model function to turn backscatter into wind speed, using streaks in the image for direction. Scored against the anemometers on 11 NOAA moored buoys off California, adjusted from 4 m to 10 m with a neutral log profile, within 30 minutes of each pass.

1.71 m/sRMSE, as the app runs it
r = 0.909correlation
1.49 m/sRMSE given the buoy's direction

shows Wind good enough to say what a miss means: a search in 3 m/s and one in 14 m/s are not the same search. 79% of readings are within 2 m/s.

does not show Unbiased wind. As the app runs it, light wind reads high (+1.2 m/s below 5 m/s); given the buoy's direction, strong wind reads low (-1.6 m/s above 10 m/s). Only the California coast was tested, and streak directions are within 30° only about half the time (25.5° median error).

Dark-vessel detector · CFAR + AIS

Ships in the radar, checked against their transponders

Ships broadcast their position on AIS; the radar sees them whether they do or not. Over 9 real Sentinel-1 windows across four days, every AIS ship the radar should have seen is a free labelled trial: an echo is a hit, silence a miss. Ships' identities are hashed and never stored.

47 / 51ships over 100 m found
37 / 54small craft under 35 m
958unexplained echoes in 5,464 km²

shows Large ships are found reliably, and the rate falls with length the way radar physics says it should. The calibration is measured per length band with honest intervals, and bands without enough ships are marked, not filled in.

does not show A low false-alarm rate. The 958 echoes with no AIS ship are platforms, small boats without transponders and false alarms together; as an upper bound that is 187 per 1,000 km² on rough-sea passes, the detector's weakest number.

Seabed relief anomalies · NOAA BlueTopo

Wrecks in survey bathymetry

NOAA charts thousands of wrecks, and the ones located by hydrographic survey have trustworthy positions. For 85 of them, 5-120 m deep in twelve survey-dense regions, the engine searched a 1.2 km BlueTopo window centred on the charted point, blind.

41 / 85found (95%: 38-59%)
9.4 mmedian position error

shows When a wreck stands out of the grid the engine finds it and puts it within about 10 m of the chart. Coarser 8 m grids found a similar share to 4 m ones.

does not show Whether the misses are the detector's fault. Many charted wrecks are buried, salvaged or too low to rise out of a 4-16 m grid; separating 'not in the data' from 'missed' is the next study.

A real loss · ARA San Juan, 2017

Rebuilding a real search from public facts

On 15 November 2017 the Argentine submarine ARA San Juan was lost in the South Atlantic. Two hydroacoustic stations heard an implosion-like event; the wreck was found a year later, 19.8 km from the published event location, 907 m down. Using only public facts (Wikipedia, citing the Argentine Navy and the CTBTO), the engine rebuilds the location as a two-station bearing fix, then adds one piece of physics: a hull implodes only after sinking past its collapse depth, so water shallower than the test depth (300 m) is ruled out, on real NOAA ETOPO 2022 bathymetry.

On land

Sentinel-2 imagery, real terrain
Spectral anomalies · Sentinel-2

Small things that are the wrong colour

A tent, a tarp, a car or a crashed aircraft is rarely the colour of the ground around it. The engine scores every pixel of a real Sentinel-2 scene by its Mahalanobis distance from the background in six bands (the Reed-Xiaoli detector), thresholds at a chi-square tail of 10⁻⁹ and drops anything bigger than twelve pixels. No public list of real tents in satellite scenes exists, so the truth is made: 2,500 targets with typical published spectra, planted at known places into real scenes from five landscapes.

123 / 12520 m² orange tents found
59 / 125at 10 m², a tenth of a pixel
~8 / km²other anomalies on real ground

shows Targets a fifth of a pixel across are found almost every time against real Sierra granite, Scottish moorland, Oregon forest and Nevada desert. White paint is hardest at small sizes, because bright rock is common.

does not show Detection of real lost targets: the spectra are assumptions and the targets single, linearly mixed pixels. One scene alone also flags about eight other things per km² (rock, roofs, vehicles), so on its own it is a list of places to look.

New since they went missing

Keeping only what is new

Most of those other anomalies are permanent; a lost person's tent is new. Two Sentinel-2 passes of the same tile share the exact 10 m grid, so each contact is checked against an earlier pass, chosen by how much of the ground it saw clear. Choosing it by the scene's overall cloud figure failed first: it picked April and February scenes whose ground was under snow.

How to read this page

"Real" here means real inputs and an independent truth, not operational use: ABYSSAL has not been used in a live search. The sea studies use found truth (buoys, transponders, charts, a wreck); the land studies use made truth (targets planted in real scenes), which tests the detector against real backgrounds but not against real lost things. Every number regenerates from its script; the receipts are in the ABYSSAL repository under abyssal/realdata/results, with the write-up in docs/validation/REAL_DATA.md.
