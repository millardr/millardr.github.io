I studied Engineering Science at the University of Oxford, graduating in 2026 with a First and ranked first overall in my cohort. My work there ranged from high-dimensional optimisation and mathematical modelling to Bayesian estimation and the robustness of machine-learning systems; my master's research developed efficient methods for optimising supersonic nozzle geometries.

What connects all of it is a question I keep coming back to: how sure should we be? A search map that says 80%, a detector that says "ship", a model that says 90%. Each of those numbers drives a decision, and each is only as good as the reasoning and the measurements behind it.

My largest project, ABYSSAL, is a Bayesian workbench for finding lost aircraft, ships and people. It is built around that question: empty searches count as evidence, correlated reports count once, detectors are calibrated before they are believed, and every number keeps a record of where it came from.

I'm interested in roles and collaborations where that kind of rigour matters: search and rescue, maritime and aerospace sensing, autonomy, scientific computing, AI evaluation and national security. If your work is in one of those, I would be glad to talk.
