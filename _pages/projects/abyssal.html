ABYSSAL began with a simple question: if you have looked for something and failed to find it, what have you actually learned?

Over about a month it has grown into a full spatial reasoning workstation: a double-precision log-space Bayesian engine with six documented model versions; provenance-root pooling that refuses to count echoes twice; an Evidence Map that measures each item's and each source's influence in bits; a live search loop with exact undo; multi-asset planning with expected value, break-even and search-to-exhaustion stopping curves; a multi-target watchlist; an Ops console with live air and sea traffic, satellites, weather, terrain and field tools; and a Signal Lab of physics-screened detectors for radar wakes, hydrophone arrivals and debris rings.

It is a prototype. It runs on one machine, it is backed by roughly 2,500 Python tests, 330 frontend unit tests and 118 browser journeys, and it has not yet been validated against real operational outcomes. Every detection probability in it is labelled an analyst assumption until measured.
